Benchless

You don't need a lab to publish your first paper.

Thousands of engineering students are told their research career is over before it starts, because their university has no equipment, no funding, and no one with time to supervise them. That is a resourcing problem, not a talent problem — and simulation solves it.

Benchless walks you from "I have a laptop and an idea" to a submitted first-author paper at a conference that actually counts. It was built by someone who did exactly that: an undergraduate in Dhaka, no lab access, accepted to IEEE EDTM.

What it does

  1. Scopes a study you can actually finish. You describe your field, your background and what software you can get. It proposes simulation-based studies that are genuinely novel, genuinely runnable on your hardware, and sized to the time you have.
  2. Checks the venue before you waste a year on it. Predatory conferences and journals take your money and quietly damage your applications. Every venue is checked against public indexing records, and the reasoning is shown to you.
  3. Gives you the structure. A submission timeline with real dates, the sections a reviewer expects in your subfield, and a LaTeX kit that matches the venue's template.

What it will never do is write your paper. There is a whole industry selling authorship — ghostwritten manuscripts placed in compromised journals for a fee. That is fraud, it is detectable, and it ends careers. Benchless teaches you the method and checks your work. The research is yours, the writing is yours, and your name on it means something.
